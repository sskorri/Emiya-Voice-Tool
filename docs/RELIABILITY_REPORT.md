## Voice storage and crash recovery

A bake now writes its conditioning into a private staging file. Publication takes the registry file lock and prepares the new registry image alongside a recoverable copy of the old conditioning. The atomic rename of the prepared registry is the commit point. After a writer is killed, opening the registry completes recovery before returning voice data. A kill before that rename restores the old pair, including the absence of both files for a new voice. A kill after the rename retains the new pair. Recovery also survives interruption during its own rollback. The decision works even if the old and new metadata are identical. [Transaction implementation](../engine/storage.py) · [Registry integration](../engine/registry.py)

A known cached voice keeps its original reference clip on a cache hit. An unregistered conditioning file, or one left by switching backends, is baked again when its provenance cannot be established. The slow bake runs outside the registry lock. Cache reuse, publication, registry edits and deletion coordinate through the lock. An incomplete copy made on a filesystem without hard-link support stays private. Before a transaction marker exists, a process kill can leave a private stage file. The normal GUI startup sweep can reclaim it once it is ten minutes old. [Engine](../engine/engine.py) · [Startup sweep](../ui/core/services/leftovers.py)

**24 confirmed writer terminations** covered first bakes, forced replacements, switches between backends, a kill partway through copying a backup, a second kill during recovery, and concurrent metadata edits. Tests checked the reference recorded in the actual conditioning bytes and the registry entry, including an unrelated voice. Real-engine orchestration used model test doubles. The same matrix exercised FakeEngine publication and reopened its persistent registry. Focused engine, registry and data-safety verification recorded **385 passed and one skipped**. The guarantee covers process termination during conditioning and registry publication. Power loss, a failing storage device, separate reference-WAV and Recycle Bin operations, and repairs to untraceable data left by older releases are outside that guarantee. [Crash tests](../tests/reliability/test_conditioning_crash.py) · [Focused results](../artifacts/reliability/2026-11-13/conditioning-transaction.xml)

## Idle rendering

The idle Speak page retained completed transform animations on two rows of 230 waveform bars. Releasing those transforms preserved their entry animation and final geometry and reduced a native diagnostic from **43.48% to 25.04% of one logical CPU** at about 60 fps. This was a 280 Hz exploratory run on the primary display. The previous 1920 × 1080, 144 Hz acceptance run measured **36.65%**, against the **14.7%** whole-process-tree budget. [Diagnostic](../artifacts/reliability/followup/paint-runs/results.json) · [Previous acceptance](../artifacts/reliability/2026-11-13/native-frozen/results.txt)

The final renderer uses the original 18 px CSS radial gradient and gives the browser compositor a track of opacity values sampled from the original easing curve. A paused take no longer needs a JavaScript callback or a canvas upload every frame to pulse. Minimize, hide, restore, Effects off and Reduce Motion retain their respective behavior. The field, its particles and the 60 fps cap are unchanged. The field replay matched **1,490,794 drawing calls over 4,684 frames**. The final renderer passed the timing and lifecycle tests at simulated 60, 144 and 280 Hz, and the focused FX and Speak suite passed **145 tests**. [Investigation and A/B runs](../artifacts/reliability/followup/paint-findings.md) · [Field parity](../artifacts/reliability/followup/field-parity.json) · [Final cadence](../artifacts/reliability/followup/compositor-cadence.json)

An independent smoke run then passed **14/14 checks**, including idle CPU at **13.54% and 60.03 fps**. Both measured runs used the same source and the 144 Hz display. The complete native suite's idle result was 20.85%, so the evidence does **not** establish that the app consistently meets 14.7%. The metric counts the app's whole process tree against one logical CPU. Dividing by this machine's 24 logical CPUs puts the observed idle range at about **0.56–0.87% of total CPU time** (the 14.7% budget corresponds to 0.61%). This is a CPU-time comparison, not a power measurement. All animation density, assets and the frame cap are retained; the threshold was neither raised nor marked as an expected failure. [Independent smoke](../artifacts/reliability/followup/native-repeat/results.txt)

A low 7.68% reading from an experimental cached brush did not reproduce under controlled replay. Across three interleaved measurements per renderer, with the same simulation state, particle trajectories and frame rate, the original field averaged **15.93%** and the two cached-brush alternatives averaged **16.06%** and **15.42%**. Expanded visual testing found channel errors as high as 55/255 for the first alternative. A pixel-identical partial-clear experiment was slower. Neither the drawing experiments nor any reduction in animation were shipped. [Measurements and visual comparison](../artifacts/reliability/followup/paint-findings.md)

## Focus protection

The exploratory profile exposed a late focus failure even though the prior native suite passed. The WinForms test window was foreground about 38.8 seconds after launch, for one approximately 51 ms sampling interval. The watcher killed its process tree. That incident remains in the diagnostic record. [Focus evidence](../artifacts/reliability/followup/paint-runs/results.json)

The native harness now starts its test processes on a private Win32 desktop. It checks desktop identity before importing the GUI, keeps the input-desktop observer separate and never switches to the test desktop. Windowless probes verified the actual child and grandchild on the private desktop and the original input desktop before and after the run. The focus, placement and harness group passed 86 checks. Its 14 native cases were excluded from that windowless check. The N5 test requires both real drawn frames and scheduler ticks near 60 Hz. The harness also checks the configured game process names in the interactive Windows session before launching a window. [Desktop isolation proof](../artifacts/reliability/2026-11-13/verification/private_desktop_windowless.json) · [Harness tests](../artifacts/reliability/2026-11-13/verification/private_desktop_tests.txt) · [Windows desktop input model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winstation/desktops)

The first private-desktop run exposed a teardown error: the monitor thread still held its desktop binding when the harness closed that desktop. The observer now restores its original thread desktop before exiting, with bounded handling for USER32's delayed release. Eight windowless create/observe/close cycles and the full native suite completed without that error. Every final native launch recorded the input desktop as Default before and after testing, with **zero foreground incidents**. ## Real models and UI lag

Three fresh real-engine end-to-end runs passed on the RTX 4070 Ti. Each loaded the three backends and generated four takes through the UI. Each accompanying soak completed nine loads and 27 takes, with never more than one model resident. The first soak's round-three drift in generation seconds per second of audio was **+98.38% multilingual, +108.18% Turbo**; the instrumented repeat measured **+102.07% and +110.44%**. Standard stayed within the +50% budget in those first two soaks. The slowdowns occurred inside token sampling. Token counts and output durations matched the successful baseline. The repeat retained 44/64/64 MiB of allocator reservations after Free VRAM. During the instrumented repeat, no timing sample showed thermal or power limiting, all 132 display/process snapshots showed one fixed 1080p monitor, and Destiny 2 was absent. The machine changed from two monitors to one between the initial measurements; that does not explain the reproduction on the fixed configuration. [Initial run](../artifacts/reliability/followup/gpu-initial/gpu_results.json) · [Instrumented run](../artifacts/reliability/followup/gpu-diagnostic/gpu_results.json) · [Timing and telemetry](../artifacts/reliability/followup/gpu-diagnostic/findings.md)

A third run traced the generation worker without changing its priority, affinity or power settings. This time round-three drift was **+3.77% multilingual, +116.44% standard and +63.43% Turbo**. Within one 756-token Turbo take, the same OS thread produced tokens at a median **9.80 ms** in intervals sampled on performance cores versus **23.17 ms** on efficiency cores. The slow Standard takes were sampled entirely on efficiency cores; every soak take in the first two rounds was sampled on performance cores. No thread-priority change, requested power throttling or material garbage-collection pause accompanied the difference. This provides strong evidence that host core placement limits GPU token submission in this background run. It does not establish foreground performance. No scheduling override or relaxed test threshold was applied. The third soak retained **64 MiB** reserved after every Free VRAM; maximum allocated after unload was **24.68 MiB**, with no resident model or engine lock held after close. [Third run](../artifacts/reliability/followup/gpu-worker-diagnostic/gpu_results.json) · [Worker samples and timing](../artifacts/reliability/followup/gpu-worker-diagnostic/worker_summary.json)

UI loop lag is measured separately from generation speed. The original 700–763 ms lag spikes came from the test's synchronous VRAM probe. Moving it off the event loop cut the isolated measurement from 739 ms to 15 ms. Across all three new end-to-end and soak pairs, no loop-lag sample exceeded 500 ms. The three soak maxima were 465, 309 and 325 ms; each soak had p95 of 13 ms.
